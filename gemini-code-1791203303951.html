The MindScape Well-being Hub is an interactive digital web application created for the Year 11 SEL project (Option 6 Digital Artefact). Designed to promote mental health literacy, emotional self-regulation, and peer solidarity, MindScape transforms passive health advice into a dynamic, multi-sensory experience for secondary school students at Garden International School.

The platform is built around core interactive pillars: a 10-Second Mood Pulse Check-In, a Guided Breathwork Engine, a 5-4-3-2-1 Sensory Grounding Tool, a 5-Point Mindful Assessment, a Live Chart.js Analytics Dashboard, an Asymmetrical Bento Photo Gallery, and a Direct Counselor Assistance Modal. The self-assessment and mood pulse tools measure stress, sleep quality, workload management, screen balance, and social habits, delivering instant score feedback alongside tailored self-care recommendations. To foster social connection, the habit leaderboard gamifies positive daily practices—such as mindfulness breaks and acts of kindness—encouraging accountability across the student body.

Visually, the site utilizes dark-mode glassmorphism, live web API quote integrations, and curated photography to establish a calm, modern environment. Coupled with WCAG-compliant ambient soundscapes (rain, waves, forest), MindScape lowers the barrier to discussing mental health, equipping students with practical, daily tools for personal and social well-being.

Developing the MindScape Hub deepened our understanding of how user-centered digital design can enhance health communication and community well-being. Throughout the development process, we learned that engaging young people in mental health practices requires interactive, non-judgmental platforms that offer immediate value—such as streak tracking, guided breathwork pacing, and personalized self-care insights.

Balancing technical functionality with meaningful SEL content taught us how environmental factors—like visual hierarchy, color psychology, and soundscapes—directly impact emotional engagement. This project reinforced our belief that technology, when designed with empathy, can serve as a catalyst for building supportive, resilient school communities.
